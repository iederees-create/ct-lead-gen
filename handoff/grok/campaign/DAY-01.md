## 1. Campaign objective

Make builders feel seen: vague “how much?” messages are not usable quote requests — and they burn time, trust, and good leads.

---

## 2. Core story

Builders open WhatsApp hoping for work. They get:

> “Hi, how much to renovate my house?”

The customer wants an instant number. The builder needs ten follow-ups. Half the threads die before room sizes, finishes, or access exist. The cost isn’t just unpaid admin — it’s false starts, bad pricing pressure, and silent drop-offs.

---

## 3. Main hook

**“Hi, how much to renovate my house?”**  
That’s not a quote request. That’s a conversation with almost no data.

## 4. Facebook personal-profile post

Every builder knows this message.

“Hi, how much to renovate my house?”

No suburb details that matter.  
No size.  
No finish level.  
No “are we moving walls or just cabinets?”  
No photos.  
No timeline.

Just a price request — and an expectation that you should already know the answer.

So you ask the same ten questions again.  
Some people answer.  
Most disappear halfway through.

That’s not a bad customer.  
That’s a bad **intake system**.

This week I’m walking through a better quote-request experience for construction businesses — starting with why vague enquiries cost more than they look.

## 7. LinkedIn post

In construction, a one-line price question is often treated as a “lead.”

Operationally, it’s closer to an incomplete job file.

Without scope, measurements, finish level, trade involvement, access, and timeline, estimators are forced into either:

1. endless clarification threads, or  
2. premature numbers that become disputes later.

Neither is a marketing win. Both are process debt.

This week I’m publishing a short series on designing construction websites that collect **build-ready enquiry structure** before the first WhatsApp reply.

Day 1: why “how much to renovate?” is not a quote request.

If you run a building or renovation company, you’ll recognise the pattern immediately.
